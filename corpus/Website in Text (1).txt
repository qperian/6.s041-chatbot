Kindergarten (K2)
K2 Kindergarten is a six-hour (full-day) program for 5-year-olds and is provided in all BPS elementary schools and early learning centers. K2 programs are a critical part of a child's early school success. School assignment is guaranteed for all children who apply for K2. However, due to space constraints, including the reality that most K1 students moving up into K2 seats in their same school, an assignment cannot be guaranteed to a specific school. Please be sure to inquire during School Choice season about the number of open seats anticipated, and please register in the month of January for the best chance at securing an assignment in a school of your choosing. This is called Round One registration. To register for K2, children must be 5-years old on or before September 1 of the school year for which they are registering. Visit the Welcome Services Registration webpage to learn more about school registration. [https://www.bostonpublicschools.org/fs/pages/2737]
Get ready for your "Countdown to Kindergarten"
Countdown to Kindergarten is a partnership BPS leads with the City of Boston, and many community organizations, which aims to provide a positive and supportive transition into BPS kindergarten (K0, K1 or K2) for students and their families. The initiative offers free parent-child playgroups, provides readiness tips and opportunities, and hosts workshops and other transition activities. Pick up copies of their publications, available in many languages, at any Welcome Center, call 617-635-9288, visit their Facebook page, or explore their website: [www.countdowntokindergarten.org]
Important UPK Notes
* All kindergarten students are guaranteed a seat in their program through the highest grade offered in their assigned school, following the guidelines of our student assignment plan.

* Exceptions cannot be made to our entry age policy, regardless of the child’s previous school experience.

* State law states each child must attend school beginning in September of the calendar year in which the child turns six-years old.

* Suppose you need a program for your three- or four-year-old. In that case, you are encouraged to explore the new Great Starts registration tool [https://www.bostonpublicschools.org/fs/pages/2751], a collaboration with the BPS, the City of Boston, and community-based early childhood programs, designed to support Boston PreK in the city of Boston. While Pre-K programming in BPS has expanded in recent years, assignment in one of our schools cannot be guaranteed.

Elementary Schools
Most BPS elementary schools are PK or K to sixth grade; however, a few schools are K-8 in grade span. The district also has several Early Childhood options, with grade ranges spanning K0 to Grade 1, 2, or 3. Please be sure to review grade spans when you consider school options for your child.
Rising Grade 7 students in a K-6 school will receive a school choice form to make new school choices for a district high school, which typically spans grades 7 through 12. Note, all schools that serve grades 7-12 are citywide options that every student has access to, regardless of where they live in the city.
To see your full school choice list, including locations, schedules, and other information, visit [www.discoverbps.org]. You can also contact one of our Welcome Centers for assistance [https://www.bostonpublicschools.org/fs/pages/2738].

High Schools
Boston Public Schools offers a number of different high school options, each of which includes a wide variety of programs. Most of our high schools serve grades 7 through 12, and all BPS high schools are city-wide, which means that every student living in Boston is eligible to apply for any BPS high school. Please note, however, that some schools do have special admission requirements. Below is more information on how to find schools that are a good fit for you and how to apply to those schools.
Where can I learn about each of these schools?
For information about BPS Secondary Schools, also known as High Schools, please visit the GUIDE TO HIGH SCHOOL CHOICE [https://www.bostonpublicschools.org/fs/resource-manager/view/ead50d8f-1d54-4e02-9ef3-aa2c21867f3b]. This is an excellent tool to learn about the types of offerings available to students. Or, to learn more about your options:
   1. Go to DiscoverBPS.org and sort your school options by the programs and school features that best fit your needs.

   2. Explore each school’s website - BPS School Directory [https://www.bostonpublicschools.org/fs/pages/1634].

   3. Visit each school in person or remotely during School Preview Times [https://www.bostonpublicschools.org/fs/pages/2739], or contact each school directly by phone or email to schedule other tour times .

How many schools should I list on my registration form?
More is better. At a minimum, we suggest you list five schools in the order in which you would like to attend, but we encourage you to rank as many schools as possible. Listing more schools does not harm your chances of getting into your first choice school.
Our assignment process works to provide your child with a seat at the school of your highest ranking, if there is an available seat.
However, selections are done in random order, so it is important to list all the schools for which you are interested, in case there are no open seats at your top choices.
What does “Additional Application Requirements” or “Special Admissions” mean?
Certain schools require students to submit an extra application to be considered for admission. These applications can sometimes include an essay, teacher recommendations, and/or an audition. Contact the school directly to learn about their application process, or explore more information at https://www.discoverbps.org. Note: If you do not submit a separate application to these schools, you will not be considered for admission.
Schools requiring special application processes include:
      * Boston Arts Academy

      * Boston Day and Evening Academy

      * Boston Green Academy

      * Edward M. Kennedy Academy for Health Careers

      * Fenway High School

      * New Mission High School

      * Madison Park Technical Vocational High School

The Special Application Schools for the 2025-2026 School Year contains the specific requirements for each school and their application deadlines for the 2024-2025 school year [https://drive.google.com/file/d/1-gpDfv0R-Qi_0I8UdcxKrF6Z7rfP-SjO/view]. The second page contains the link to the school's online application.
How is the choice process different for schools with additional requirements?
Although each of these schools has its own application, families must also rank them on their BPS School Choice form, along with their other choices in the order of preference.
Boston Public Schools assignment process works to provide students and families with their highest ranked option to which seats are available; however, because we cannot guarantee a specific assignment, it will always benefit you to rank your choices honestly.
It is important to note that there is no advantage to ranking special admissions schools last. It will actually put at risk any possible assignment to a school at which you were accepted.
This is because our system aims to place students in their highest ranked schools – and then confirm their acceptance to any special admission schools – before finalizing the assignment.
What about the exam schools?
BPS has three exam schools:
         * Boston Latin Academy

         * Boston Latin School

         * O’Bryant School of Math & Science

Learn more about the exam schools and their unique admission process and timeline here: [https://www.bostonpublicschools.org/exam]
What about charter schools?
There are three in-district charter schools that serve students in grades 9-12:
            * Boston Day and Evening Academy

            * Boston Green Academy

            * Edward M. Kennedy Academy for Health Careers

These schools are shown on your choice list but also require an additional application to each school.
There are additional charter schools in and around Boston that will not appear on your choice list. These schools are not part of Boston Public Schools and have their own enrollment processes. If you are interested in one of these schools, please visit [https://www.applybostoncharterschools.org].
When will I be informed of my assigned school?
Students entering grade 9 that participate in the priority registration period and submit their school choices by the beginning of February will receive their school assignment by the end of March. Students that are entering grades 7, 10, 11, and 12 or students that miss out on the priority registration period for grade 9 will be part of the second registration period and will find out their school assignment by the end of May.
Please note that if your child requires a full inclusion or substantially separate placement, some of this information may not apply. Please contact the Office of Special Education at 617-635-8599 if you have any questions.
What is Boston Public School's policy on International Exchange Students?
If you are an international student looking to enroll in BPS as part of an exchange program or on a temporary basis, you must be enrolled in grade 9, 10, or 11, and the duration of your enrollment in BPS cannot exceed one academic year. Learn more about BPS's policy around international exchange students: https://www.bostonpublicschools.org/Page/6739
